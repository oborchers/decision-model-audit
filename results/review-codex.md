## Referee review: five corrections, ranked

1. **High confidence: remove the claim of accuracy parity.** The report says Jev “is as accurate as” the LLMs because Holm adjusted tests are non-significant. Those tests do not establish equivalence. Sonnet scores 359/400 versus Jev’s 345/400; its reported paired difference is +3.5 points with an unadjusted bootstrap interval of +0.8 to +6.5 points. State that this sample does not resolve the adjusted comparisons, and give the differences. [REPORT.md](results/REPORT.md:7), [REPORT.md](results/REPORT.md:42)

2. **High confidence: remove the claimed accuracy ceiling.** Five systems being wrong on the same 18 papers, including 16 with the same alternative label, does not show that the author’s category cannot be predicted. The report provides no independent adjudication of those papers. The “practical ceiling is below 100%” is an inference from model agreement, not a measured bound. The majority vote is also sensitive to an unspecified tie rule: two items tie for the most votes, and its accuracy can be 360/400 or 361/400. [REPORT.md](results/REPORT.md:145), [report.py](src/dma/analysis/report.py:205)

3. **High confidence: qualify the open-model comparison by the tested interfaces.** Qwen is measured by the next option-letter token, while the LLMs receive structured output and the decision models use their own interfaces. Qwen’s first option receives 182/400 S1 predictions; reversing the labels changes accuracy from 53.25% to 48.5%. SemIf’s 77.5% with the same Qwen base further shows how much the readout matters. “Every open model tested” is defensible only as a claim about these specific configurations. “All systems receive the same label names and descriptions” also needs qualification: NLI passes descriptions as hypotheses, and GLiNER removes parentheses. [REPORT.md](results/REPORT.md:8), [local.py](src/dma/runners/local.py:267), [local.py](src/dma/runners/local.py:414)

4. **High confidence: record the unlogged protocol changes and correct the completion claim.** The frozen protocol specifies a multiclass Brier score, 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples, 400 training items per S1 class, and a fair-die P1 template. The implementation uses top-label Brier, 2,000 or 5,000 resamples, 1,698 training items in total, and a wheel template. The protocol also promises unambiguous S1 results; they are calculated in `summary.json` but absent from the report. These departures are not in the changelog. [protocol.md](notes/protocol.md:28), [protocol.md](notes/protocol.md:72), [report.py](src/dma/analysis/report.py:73), [build_probes.py](src/dma/data/build_probes.py:21), [data_quality.json](results/data_quality.json)

5. **Medium confidence: revise calibration and rationale interpretations.** ECE’s docstring says ties stay together, but equal-mass slicing splits them according to stable input order. For S1 Jev, the reported ECE is 0.0643; allocating tied groups fractionally across bins gives 0.0676. The resulting ECE depends partly on row order, especially with Jev’s 206 exact 1.0 confidences. The rationale runs also change instructions and maximum output tokens from 150 to 600, so the observed 4.5 to 7 point loss belongs to that *rationale prompt condition*, not to writing a rationale alone. [metrics.py](src/dma/analysis/metrics.py:35), [api.py](src/dma/runners/api.py:88), [REPORT.md](results/REPORT.md:12)

**Independent raw-row checks:** Jev S1 accuracy is 345/400 = 86.25%; Luna is 355/400 = 88.75%; Jev’s error at 80% coverage is 8.59%; Jev has 206/400 exact 1.0 confidences; and Jev chooses “none” on 95/100 P4 items and 4/400 S1 items. These reproduce the rounded headlines. The paired Jev–Luna discordances are 14 Jev-only and 24 Luna-only, giving the reported +2.5 points. [REPORT.md](results/REPORT.md:40), [REPORT.md](results/REPORT.md:84)

**Other statistical and code checks:** Wilson, exact McNemar, Holm, and paired item bootstrap implementations appear correct. Tie-aware risk at coverage and the reported AURC reproduce from raw rows; Jev’s risk is flat through **51.5%**, not about 60%. Deduplication’s last-row rule produced no valid-to-invalid reversals in the main files; the latency merge applies to 2,400 S1 and 1,074 S2 duplicate keys. Holm adjustment nevertheless includes rationale variants in the choice-comparison family, contrary to the protocol’s “across systems” wording. Choice-only adjustment would change Sonnet’s Holm p from 0.170 to 0.122 without changing the significance conclusion.
